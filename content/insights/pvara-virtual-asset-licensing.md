Pakistan now has a dedicated regulator and licensing regime for virtual assets. Here is how the framework came together in 2026.

## How it came together

<ul class="timeline">
<li><span class="when">March 2026</span><span>Parliament passes the Virtual Assets Act, 2026, making the Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA) a permanent statutory body.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">14 April 2026</span><span>State Bank Circular No. 10 of 2026 lets regulated entities open accounts for licensed virtual asset service providers, including segregated client money accounts. It replaces a prohibition in place since 2018.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">11 June to 2 July 2026</span><span>Public consultation on the draft regulations.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">21 August 2026</span><span>PVARA notifies its licensing regulations and opens its licensing portal.</span></li>
<li><span class="when">5 September 2026</span><span>Deadline for existing operators to apply for a No-Objection Certificate, or stop operating.</span></li>
</ul>

## Ten licence categories

- Exchange
- Custody
- Broker-dealer
- Advisory
- Lending and borrowing
- Derivatives
- Asset management
- Transfer and settlement
- Issuance
- Mining-related services

Each category has its own rules on conduct, prudential requirements, technology and anti-money laundering.

## Protections for customers

- Licensed providers must keep customer holdings separate from their own funds.
- They cannot lend or pledge customer holdings without written consent.
- Customer holdings must be protected if the platform fails.

## Two routes in

Firms that plan to incorporate in Pakistan can start in a sandbox or obtain a No-Objection Certificate first, then apply for a full licence once incorporated.

<p class="pull">Banks can now bank licensed providers, <em>for the first time since 2018.</em></p>
